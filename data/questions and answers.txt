q001 (MSFT Revenue YoY Growth)
Question: By how much did Microsoft's total revenue change from FY2023 to FY2024, and what principal growth drivers did management identify in MD&A?

Answer:
Total revenue increased $33.2 billion, or 16%, from $211,915,000,000 in FY2023 to $245,122,000,000 in FY2024.

Per Item 7 MD&A (FY2024), management attributed growth across all three segments:
- Intelligent Cloud — driven by Azure
- Productivity and Business Processes — driven by Office 365 Commercial
- More Personal Computing — driven by Gaming

q003 (MSFT Operating Income & Drivers)
Question: How much did Microsoft's operating income increase between FY2023 and FY2024, and what key operating expense trends explained this margin shift?

Answer:
Operating income increased $20.9 billion, or 24%, from $88,523,000,000 (FY2023) to $109,433,000,000 (FY2024).

Key operating expense trends (Item 7 MD&A, FY2024):
- Total operating expenses increased $4.0 billion (7%), driven by Gaming (including Activision Blizzard) and cloud engineering investments, partially offset by the prior-year Q2 charge
- Research and development increased $2.3 billion (9%), driven by Gaming (7 points from Activision Blizzard) and cloud engineering investments
- Sales and marketing increased $1.7 billion (7%), driven by Gaming (6 points from Activision Blizzard)
- General and administrative increased slightly; Activision Blizzard growth was offset in part by the prior-year Q2 charge

Operating income growth was driven by growth across each of the three segments.

Sources: xbrl_facts.csv (operating_income rows); msft_fy2024_10k.htm Item 7

q004 (Fiscal Period Probe — MSFT vs AAPL Reporting Windows)
Question: What was the difference in total operating cash flow between Microsoft and Apple in FY2024, and how do their underlying fiscal period calendar lengths and period end dates differ?

Answer:
Operating cash flow difference: Microsoft was higher by $294,000,000.
- MSFT FY2024: $118,548,000,000
- AAPL FY2024: $118,254,000,000

Fiscal period differences:
- MSFT FY2024: period end 2024-06-30; fiscal window Jul 2023 – Jun 2024; standard 12-month year with fixed June 30 year-end
- AAPL FY2024: period end 2024-09-28; fiscal window Oct 2023 – Sep 2024; 52-week year ending the last Saturday of September

These are different reporting windows that overlap by only about three quarters. AAPL uses a 52- or 53-week fiscal calendar; MSFT does not have a 52/53-week effect.

q013 (MSFT Item 1)
Question: How does Microsoft describe its business segment alignment and primary product lines in Item 1 of its FY2024 Form 10-K?

Answer:
Microsoft operates and reports financial performance using three segments aligned with its product portfolio:

q014 (MSFT Item 1A)
Question: What specific cybersecurity and operational risks does Microsoft highlight in Item 1A of its FY2024 Form 10-K?

Answer:
Key cybersecurity and operational risks include:

Cybersecurity:
- Cyberattacks and security vulnerabilities from hackers, sophisticated organizations, and nation-states
- Unauthorized access to IT systems and customer data (including the late-2023 nation-state password spray incident)
- Security of products, services, devices, and customers' data — including cloud and on-premises vulnerabilities, zero-day exploits, and generative-AI security threats
- Inadequate account security, IT supply chain attacks, and evolving threats using AI
- Need to continuously engineer secure products, deploy patches, and maintain defensive measures

Operational:
- Excessive outages, data losses, and disruptions of online services if operations infrastructure is inadequate
- Datacenter dependency on energy, networking supplies, servers, and GPUs — especially with growing AI demand
- Quality or supply problems — limited suppliers for device and datacenter components, industry shortages, supply chain disruptions in Asia affecting datacenter servers, Xbox, and Surface hardware

Source: msft_fy2024_10k.htm Item 1A

q016 (AAPL Item 1)
Question: How does Apple describe its supply chain and manufacturing operations in Item 1 of its FY2024 Form 10-K?

Answer:
Apple depends on outsourcing partners, many outside the U.S., for component manufacturing and logistical services. Substantially all manufacturing is performed in whole or in part by partners located primarily in China mainland, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and Vietnam, with significant concentration among a small number of partners, often in single locations. Apple has also outsourced much of its transportation and logistics management. It relies on single-source outsourcing partners in the U.S., Asia, and Europe for many components, and on partners primarily in Asia for final assembly of substantially all hardware products. Apple has invested in manufacturing process equipment held at certain partners and made prepayments under long-term supply agreements.

Source: aapl_fy2024_10k.htm Item 1 (Supply of Components; Risk Factors cross-reference)

q017 (AAPL Item 1A)
Question: What risks related to international trade and tariffs does Apple detail in Item 1A of its FY2024 Form 10-K?

Answer:
Apple states that restrictions on international trade — such as tariffs and other controls on imports or exports of goods, technology, or data — can materially adversely affect its business and supply chain, particularly when they apply to countries and regions where Apple derives significant revenue or has significant supply chain operations. Changing the business and supply chain in response to new or changed trade restrictions can be expensive, time-consuming, and disruptive. Trade disputes and conflicts can escalate; for example, tensions between the U.S. and China have led to tariffs and restrictions affecting Apple's business, and further escalation could have significantly more severe effects.

q019 (MSFT Item 1A — FY23 vs FY24)
Question: What specific AI regulation and hardware supply constraints did Microsoft add to its Item 1A risk factors in FY2024 compared to FY2023?

Answer:
AI regulation (added or expanded in FY2024):
- FY2024 explicitly cites the EU AI Act and the U.S. AI Executive Order as sources of legal/regulatory risk in the "Issues in the development and use of AI" risk factor
- FY2023 referred to an EU AI Act "being considered"; FY2024 treats enacted/active regulation as a present risk
- Expanded discussion of copyright, liability, and regulatory action related to AI
- New standalone risk factor on evolving sustainability regulatory requirements (FY2024 only)

Hardware supply constraints (added or expanded in FY2024):
- FY2024 "quality or supply problems" risk now emphasizes growing demand for AI services, limited suppliers for datacenter components, competition for GPUs and hardware components, and inadequate datacenter capacity
- FY2023 version focused more on hardware product defects (Xbox, Surface) rather than AI-driven datacenter/server supply constraints

Sources: msft_fy2023_10k.htm Item 1A; msft_fy2024_10k.htm Item 1A

q020 (AAPL Item 1 — FY23 vs FY24)
Question: How did Apple's Item 1 disclosure regarding third-party application stores change between FY2023 and FY2024?

Answer:
FY2023: Apple described distributing third-party apps through the App Store and noted expected future business changes to comply with the EU Digital Markets Act by March 2024. It referenced prior changes allowing developers to communicate with consumers outside the App Store regarding alternative purchasing mechanisms, and anticipated future changes to distribution outside the App Store.

FY2024: Apple reports implemented changes rather than only anticipated ones:
- U.S.: Changes to how developers communicate with consumers within apps on the U.S. iOS and iPadOS App Store storefront regarding alternative purchasing mechanisms
- EU (DMA compliance): Changes to iOS, iPadOS, the App Store, and Safari including new business terms, alternative fee structures, alternative methods of distribution for iOS/iPadOS apps, alternative payment processing, and additional tools and APIs for developers

The FY2024 disclosure reflects actual DMA implementation and U.S. storefront policy changes, not just forward-looking compliance expectations.

Sources: aapl_fy2023_10k.htm Item 1; aapl_fy2024_10k.htm Item 1

q022 (Fiscal Period Probe — Apple 52/53 Week Cycle)
Question: How does Apple define its fiscal calendar structure in Item 7, and how many weeks were included in its FY2024 vs FY2023 fiscal years?

Answer:
Apple's fiscal year is the 52- or 53-week period that ends on the last Saturday of September. An additional week is included in the first fiscal quarter every five or six years to realign fiscal quarters with calendar quarters; this occurred in the first quarter of 2023.

- FY2024: 52 weeks
- FY2023: 53 weeks
- FY2022: 52 weeks (also stated for context)

Source: aapl_fy2024_10k.htm Item 7 (Fiscal Period)
